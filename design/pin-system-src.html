The part that earns the click

Writing the headline

The first draft of these named the problem and stopped. "Tight hips after every long run" is a description — accurate, and no reason to do anything. On a surface where the only three options are scroll, save and click, a description gets scrolled. A headline has to carry an outcome, a cost in time, or a removed objection. Ideally two of the three.

1 · The exact moment

Name the minute the reader feels it, not the body part.

“The hip you notice at mile four”

Nobody thinks "my tensor fasciae latae is short." They think about mile four. Specificity is what makes a stranger feel recognised.

2 · The time contrast

Put the cost of the fix next to the size of the problem.

“Eight hours sitting. Three minutes back.”

The ratio does the persuading — no adjective required, which is exactly why it suits a voice that doesn't oversell.

3 · The one-thing promise

One pose, one hold, one outcome. Not a class.

“One pose for the hip that shortens your stride”

A small ask converts; a whole practice is a decision, and decisions get postponed. The class can come after the click.

4 · The unexpected cause

Reframe a problem the reader already has.

“Can’t sleep? Your hips are still at work”

Opens a loop only the click can close. Use sparingly, and only where the reframe is actually true — a false one costs more than it earns.

5 · The objection killer

Say the "no" out loud before the reader thinks it.

“The recovery pose that asks nothing”

The barrier to yin is never difficulty — it's "I'm not flexible" and "I don't have time". Answer one of those in the headline and the pin does two jobs.

The rules

  • A number wherever one is true. "Three minutes" beats "a few minutes".
  • Second person. Your hip, your run, your chest.
  • Moments, not anatomy. 3pm, mile four, the evening after a long run.
  • Never a health claim. "Easier miles tomorrow", not "prevents injury".
  • No hype vocabulary. Unlock, transform, secret, ultimate, game-changer.
  • The proof line answers the headline. Promise two minutes, then say what two minutes buys.

The constraint nobody can see from the design

Two separate photo problems, two separate fixes

Geometry

Every pose photo is 3:2 landscape with Katie small and low in a wide room. A full-bleed 2:3 crop keeps 44% of the frame width; square keeps 67%; a 1.6:1 window keeps 93%.

Fix: full-bleed and the arch are allowed only for poses whose shape survives a vertical crop. Lying poses go in a band or a window.

Resolution

public/poses/*.jpg are 1400 × 933. A tight crop from those upscales and goes soft — which is exactly what the earlier full-bleed mockups show. The originals in docs/ are 5760 × 3840, 144 of them.

Fix: the pin renderer sources from the originals. Even a torso crop then lands near 1900px wide for a pin that needs 1000.

The allowlist

Full-bleed and arch — upright, kneeling and seated only:

Dragon · Twisted Dragon · Camel · Saddle · Squat · Dangling · Butterfly · Half Butterfly · Caterpillar · Dragonfly · Shoelace · Swan · Deer · Easy Pose · Cow Face · Toe Squat · Ankle Stretch · Frog · Eagle · Bow Tie

Focal points

Every crop keeps Katie's head and heart in the upper-middle third, and no text ever crosses her face. The focal value is per photo, not per template — it lives beside the image path and travels with it.

Pin identity

Every pin is one triple:

{ item, template, tone }

That triple resolves to a deterministic /pin/card.png URL, so the calendar never stores an image — it stores a plan, and the image is generated on demand. Change a template and every future pin updates itself.

How big the inventory is

37 photographed poses × 2 audience angles × 4 valid templates × 2 tones = 592, plus routines, videos and the offer pin.

At two a day that is roughly nine months before any pin identity comes round again — and Pinterest only ever sees a fresh image against each URL, which is the thing it rewards.

The rotation rules

  • Two pins a day, on different boards.
  • No destination URL twice in 14 days.
  • No pin identity twice in 180 days.
  • One in five is the offer pin — it is the one that grows the list.
  • One in five is Watch with me, pointing at a video page.
  • Seeded by date, so reloading never reshuffles the plan.

Board map

The audience tag is the board. That is what makes the mapping automatic:

  • for runners → Yoga for Runners
  • if you sit all day → Yoga for Desk Workers (new — seed it now)
  • for restless nights → Yin Yoga for Sleep & Relaxation
  • for beginners → Yin Yoga for Beginners
  • for tight hips / hamstrings → Yoga for Flexibility & Mobility
  • no tag → Yin Yoga Poses & Cues, or Routines & Sequences

The one thing standing in the way

The calendar can pick the pose, the template, the tone, the board and the destination on its own. It cannot write the benefit headline — and that copy does not exist anywhere in the repo yet. The benefits field on each pose is written for someone already reading the page ("a deep hip opener that lets gravity draw the front hip into external rotation"), not for someone scrolling past.

So each pose needs a small block of pin copy in its frontmatter:

pin_angles:
  - audience: "for runners"
    headline: "The hip you notice at mile four"     # 31 / 44
    proof:    "Three minutes a side. Gravity does it, not you."
  - audience: "if you sit all day"
    headline: "Eight hours sitting. Three minutes back."
    proof:    "One pose, both sides, on the floor beside your desk."

Two angles per pose, 37 poses — 74 headlines. That is the real work in this project, and it is the part only Katie's voice can finish. I can draft all 74 from the existing benefits and target areas so she is editing rather than writing; a build guard can then fail any pose whose angles are missing or over the character limits, so a broken pin can never reach the calendar.

What happens to /pins

It stays. /pins becomes the browse-everything view — every pin the system can make, sorted by content item, for when Katie wants something specific. /pincalendar is the opinionated one: today, tomorrow, the next seven days, two pins each, already decided. Both read the same inventory, and both stay noindex.

Handoff

Order to build

1 · The copy layer

Add pin_angles to the pose schema, draft 74 angles, Katie edits. Nothing else can start until this exists — it is the input to everything.

2 · High-res sourcing

Generate 2400px pin-grade crops from the docs/ originals into public/poses/pin/. Only then does the full-bleed hook look the way it does in the mockups.

3 · The templates

Eight functions in netlify/functions/lib/pin.mjs, extending the existing pal(tone) pattern. Window and Card first — between them they cover every pose, photographed or not.

4 · The calendar

/pincalendar over the inventory, with the rotation rules, save buttons and a done-state in localStorage. /pins rebuilt on the same inventory.

Satori limits that shape all eight

Flexbox only — no grid, no float. Absolute positioning, border-radius, linear-gradient, object-fit/object-position, opacity and hairline borders are the whole toolkit. No filters, blend modes, masks or text effects.

Text cannot shrink to fit, which is why every slot above has a hard character count and the build fails loudly rather than overflowing quietly.
