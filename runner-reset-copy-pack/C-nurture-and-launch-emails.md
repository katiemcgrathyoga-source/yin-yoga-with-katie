### Email 3 · Day 6 — a quick win

**Subject:** Try this tonight (two minutes, on your bed)
**Preview:** The smallest useful thing I can teach a runner.

Hi {$name|default:"there"},

Here's the smallest useful thing I can teach a runner, and you can do it tonight, on your bed, in your pyjamas.

Lie on your back, hug your right knee in, then let it fall across your body to the left — a reclined twist. Arms wide, shoulders heavy, and just stay there. Two minutes. Don't pull, don't adjust, don't achieve anything. Somewhere around the ninety-second mark you'll usually feel the low back and outer hip quietly give up their grip — that's the moment a 30-second stretch never reaches. Then the other side.

That little letting-go is the whole idea of Yin. Time, not force.

If you've been doing your Post-Run Reset, you've already felt it. If you haven't yet — no guilt, ever — the twist tonight counts as your start.

Take good care of yourself,
Katie

### Email 4 · Day 9 — the flexibility objection

**Subject:** "I'm the least flexible person I know"
**Preview:** Good. You're exactly who Yin is for.

Hi {$name|default:"there"},

Runners tell me this all the time, usually as a confession: *"I can't even touch my toes."*

Can I let you off that hook? Yin has nothing to do with how far you fold. There's no shape to achieve and nobody watching. You go to about 60–80% of your capacity — the point where you feel a dull, workable stretch and nothing more — and then you simply stay, and let the shape soften around you. A stiff runner two inches into a fold, breathing quietly, is doing Yin perfectly. A dancer forcing themselves flat isn't.

If anything, tight runners feel the benefit *faster*, because there's more held tension ready to let go.

So if "I'm not flexible" has been the reason you don't stretch — it was never a real reason. It's the qualification.

Take good care of yourself,
Katie
