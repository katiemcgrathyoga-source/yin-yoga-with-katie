# Katie's Voice — the yardstick for all copy

> Distilled from Katie's real spoken class transcripts and her own video descriptions.
> Match this. The persuasion in the sales copy must live *inside* this voice.
> One-line north star: **a calm, kind friend guiding you — inviting, never instructing;
> reassuring, never hyping; offering options, never promises.**

## Tone & posture
Warm & welcoming ("you are very welcome," "hi everyone"). Permission-giving / non-prescriptive — everything is an option, never a demand ("you're welcome to," "if that feels good for you," "whatever feels best for you"). Unhurried / calm ("moving really slowly," "take your time"). Reassuring & shame-free ("don't worry," "that's okay," "that's normal," "there's no need to"). Gently encouraging — celebrates showing up ("well done," "the hardest part is over now that you've made it here").

## Sentence rhythm
Softly run-on, chained with "so / and / now / then" — instruction flows into reason into reassurance. Front-loads the cue, then the option, then the "why." Written copy is tighter, two-to-three-sentence paragraphs. Frequent "if… you can…" pairs. Soft hedges everywhere: "just," "a little bit," "gently," "somewhere," "about."

## Signature words & phrases
"you're welcome to…" · "if that feels good for you" / "whatever feels best for you" · "just take a moment" · "relax your jaw, soften your facial muscles" · "drop your shoulders towards the ground" · "find your breath" / "stay connected to your breath" · "your Edge — the maximum level of sensation you can feel without any pain" (she now frames this as **about 60–80% of your capacity**) · "allow the weight of your body to drop into the earth" · "we don't need to… that's not the goal" · "come as you are." Comfort words she reaches for: *gently, softly, nice, sweet, heavy, buoyant, savor, calm, unwind, release, space, opening.*

## How she opens & closes
Opens plainly and warmly: "hello everyone and welcome to…"; introduces herself simply ("my name is Katie… if you've been here before, welcome back"). Closes: "thank you so much for joining me… I hope you enjoyed it," and — signature — **"take good care of yourself."** Subscribe/ask lines are gentle and reasoned ("that really supports me in making these videos").

## How she instructs
Invitational, never commanding: "you can," "you might," "you're welcome to," "see if," "allow," "start to." Always offers a lighter and a deeper option. Constantly normalizes props and modifications, and self-corrects openly.

## Writing/punctuation habits
Plain sentence case, standard capitalization. **Em dashes** for asides. Sparing single exclamation for a genuine invitation. **No emoji.** No ALL-CAPS hype, no hashtag walls. (Some old YouTube descriptions drift into marketing — "melts away stress," "the full body stretch you've been needing." Treat those as SEO artifacts, not her true voice; the spoken transcripts are the reliable yardstick.)

## SHE NEVER DOES THIS (strip these out)
- **Hype verbs / superlatives:** "crush," "transform your life," "game-changer," "ultimate," "unlock," "supercharge," "next-level." Her strongest word is "really nice" or "deep."
- **Fake urgency / scarcity language** beyond what's genuinely true.
- **Medical / therapeutic claims:** stretches "help to release" / "may help" — never "cure," "heal," "detox," "fix," "realign."
- **Pet names / over-familiarity:** no "hey friend," "lovely," "beautiful soul." Neutral, inclusive "everyone."
- **Exclamation spam & emoji strings.**
- **Corporate / coaching filler:** "leverage," "optimize," "level up your practice," "unlock your potential."
- **Commanding the body:** no "you WILL feel," "must," "push through," "no pain no gain." The frame is always optional, gentle, led by how *you* feel.
- **Guilt / pressure to perform.** She does the opposite: "if you haven't, don't worry — you're so welcome to just do this at your own pace."
