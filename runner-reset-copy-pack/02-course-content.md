## How the library works
Two kinds of practice:
- **Short "Target" sessions** — one per body area, ~12–15 min, for a specific tightness or when time is short.
- **Long "Full Reset" sessions** — ~40 min, one or two poses per area, to cover everything.

Because Yin is held long and deep (2–5 minutes a shape), each video is a few unhurried holds, not a crammed list. Then **guided paths** tell each person exactly what to do based on their level, their need, or a calendar plan.

## The mechanism / primer — what a runner's body actually needs
Running builds fitness by loading the same chain of tissues over and over — hips, hamstrings, glutes, calves, quads, back. That repetition makes a runner strong, and it also quietly **shortens and stiffens** those exact tissues over weeks. Mobility and recovery are the neglected half of training — and where injuries quietly begin. Yin fits perfectly: long, still holds are well-suited to **improving range of motion** and to down-shifting into the body's **recovery (parasympathetic) state**.

### Claims discipline (use this exactly)
- ✅ **Safe to say:** improves flexibility & range of motion · restores ankle & hip mobility · supports recovery & relaxation · eases the tightness running builds up.
- ✕ **Do NOT claim:** "flushes toxins / lactic acid" (a myth) · "lengthens the IT band" (it doesn't stretch) · "prevents / cures injury" — keep it to *helps* · pair any health claim with a physician note.

## Target sessions — one per body area
Each has a **why** (the science-honest reason) and a persuasive **angle**.

**Hips & Hip Flexors (~12 min)** — Running flexes the hip thousands of times, and most runners also sit all day; together that shortens the hip flexors, shortens the stride, tips the pelvis forward. *Angle: "Running plus sitting equals tight hips — open them first, and your whole stride frees up."* Seq: Sphinx 2m · Dragon 2m ea · Sleeping Swan 2m ea · Reclined Twist 1m ea.

**Hamstrings & Glutes (~14 min)** — Hamstrings do the hard eccentric braking every stride and are one of the most commonly strained runner muscles; sluggish glutes dump more load on them. *Angle: "Your hamstrings do the hardest work in every stride — give them their length back."* Seq: Caterpillar 3m · Half-Butterfly 3m ea · Sleeping Swan 2m ea · Reclined Hamstring 1m ea.

**Calves, Ankles & Feet (~12 min)** — Calves and feet store/return huge energy each step and stiffen with mileage; lost ankle dorsiflexion is linked to altered mechanics and higher injury risk (force goes up into knee/hip). Almost no runner works here. *Angle: "It all starts at your feet — stiff ankles change everything above them."* Seq: Toe-Squat 2m · Ankle-Stretch 2m · Squat 3m · Dangling 2m · Half-Saddle 2m.

**Quads & Outer Hip — "the IT band one" (~14 min)** — Quads absorb load, especially downhill. "IT band syndrome" is a top runner injury, but the band is tough fascia you can't stretch; what you *can* release is the outer hip and glutes that tension it. *Angle: "You can't stretch your IT band — but you can release what pulls on it."* Seq: Half-Saddle 2m ea · Twisted Dragon 2m ea · Banana 2m ea · Reclined Twist 1m ea.

**Lower Back & Spine (~13 min)** — Every run is impact the spine absorbs; tight hip flexors pull the pelvis forward and load the low back. Gentle movement every direction helps it recover. *Angle: "Every run quietly compresses your spine. This is how you undo it."* Seq: Wind-Relieving 2m · Sphinx 2m · Child's Pose 2m · Reclined Twist 2m ea · Banana 1m ea.

**Express Full Reset (optional, ~15 min)** — One pose per area in fifteen minutes; a bridge between the shorts and the long Resets. Seq: Dragon 2m ea · Sleeping Swan 2m ea · Caterpillar 2m · Reclined Twist 1m ea.

## Full Reset sessions — everything, in one practice
Running loads a **chain**, not single muscles — a limit in one link shows up elsewhere. A Full Reset walks the whole chain and finishes deeply restful. *Angle: "Recovery isn't one muscle — it's the whole chain, plus the calm to let it happen."*
- **The Full Reset (Foundational, ~42 min)** — covers every area; the everyday all-rounder.
- **The Restorative Reset (~40 min, with props)** — gentler and more supported; for tired legs / low-energy days.
- **The Silent Reset (~45 min, minimal cues)** — the Foundational sequence held a touch longer, quiet, for silent-practice fans.

## Guided paths — so no one is left guessing
- **Start Here** — a short free video + page: what Yin is, why it helps runners, how to use the library. The friendly front door.
- **By Level** — New to Yin (Target shorts, props, 2-min holds) · Returning (a couple of shorts + one Full Reset on a rest day) · Experienced (longer holds, Full Resets).
- **By Need** — a chooser: tight hips → Hips short · after a long run → Hamstrings/Glutes or a Full Reset · rest day → any Full Reset · sore feet/calves → Calves short · race week → Race-Week Protocol · only ten minutes → any Target short.
- **Calendar Plans** — load into the on-site calendar with reminders: **The 6-Week Foundation** (on-ramp: one area a week + a Full Reset) · **The Weekly Rhythm** (a repeatable week to keep forever) · **Race Prep** (a 3–4 week taper-aware plan that eases off into race week).
