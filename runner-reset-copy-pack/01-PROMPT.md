# THE PRODUCT (see 02-course-content.md)
"The Runner's Reset" is a PRACTICE LIBRARY, not a 6-week course: 5 short "Target"
sessions (~12–15 min, one per body area — hips, hamstrings/glutes, calves &
ankles & feet, quads/outer-hip "the IT-band one," lower back), 3 long "Full Reset"
sessions (~40 min), guided paths (by level, by need, calendar plans), and bonuses
(Tight-Hip Fix, Desk-Runner's Hip Release, Race-Week Protocol, Runner's Pose
Library, Never-Skip Scheduler). It's yin yoga: long, still holds (2–5 min a shape)
that improve range of motion and shift the body into its parasympathetic recovery
state. Full structure + per-area "why" are in the attached spec — use them.

# THE MECHANISM / BIG IDEA
Running strengthens by loading the same chain of tissues over and over — which also
quietly SHORTENS and STIFFENS them; mobility + recovery is the neglected half of
training, and where niggles begin. Yin's long holds are uniquely suited to
restoring that range and down-shifting into recovery. Lead with a mechanism like
this, not vague "feel better." Sharpen (don't discard) these angles:
  • "You can't stretch your IT band — but you can release what pulls on it."
  • "Running plus sitting equals tight hips."
  • "It all starts at your feet — stiff ankles change everything above them."
  • "Recovery isn't one muscle — it's the whole chain, plus the calm to let it happen."

# NON-NEGOTIABLE VOICE & CLAIMS RULES
Katie's voice (see 08-katie-voice.md + 09-katie-voice-sample-runners-blog.md):
warm, unhurried, permission-giving, plain, lightly dry-humoured; offers options not
commands ("you can," "you might"); the edge is "about 60–80% of your capacity";
sign-off "take good care of yourself." No emoji, no exclamation spam, no pet names,
no corporate filler.
CLAIMS DISCIPLINE (critical — legal + trust):
  ✓ Safe: improves flexibility & range of motion, restores ankle/hip mobility,
    supports recovery & relaxation, eases the tightness running builds up.
  ✕ Never: "flushes toxins/lactic acid" (myth), "lengthens the IT band" (it doesn't
    stretch), "prevents/cures/fixes injury." Keep everything to "helps/eases/
    supports," and pair any health statement with a gentle "not medical advice —
    see a professional for pain" note.
