Katie teaches kindergarten when she isn't teaching yoga, and she noticed that the children in her class never sit the way adults sit. They drop to the floor straight into shapes we've all quietly lost: folded forward over crossed legs, soles of the feet together with the knees wide, sitting back between their own feet as if it were nothing.

So this class is those positions, held long enough to start getting them back.

**[Child's Pose](/poses/childs-pose)** for three minutes opens it, with a small aside worth staying for: balasana is usually translated as child's pose, but it's closer to *embryo* pose — so you're going back further than childhood.

**[Butterfly](/poses/butterfly)** for three minutes, nothing forced down, then wipers to release it. **[Saddle](/poses/saddle)** is the one that stings adult pride — the sitting-between-the-feet position five-year-olds hold for an hour — and it's built up on blocks or cushions, or done one leg at a time.

**[Happy Baby](/poses/happy-baby)** comes with explicit permission to rock side to side rather than hold still, and a **[reclined twist](/poses/reclined-twist)** for two minutes each way brings things level again.

It ends curled on your side in the fetal position rather than flat in Corpse. If you'd rather have Corpse, Katie says take Corpse.
