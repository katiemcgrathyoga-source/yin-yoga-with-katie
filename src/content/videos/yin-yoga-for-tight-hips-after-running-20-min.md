A 20-minute Yin class for runners, and really only three shapes.

**[Dragon](/poses/dragon)** is the front of the hip — the hip flexors that every stride keeps a little short, and that sitting down afterwards keeps shorter still. Three minutes a side, as high or as low as your hips ask for that day.

**[Sleeping Swan](/poses/sleeping-swan)** is the other half: the glutes and the deep outer hip. Three minutes again, folded forward and resting, with a block under the hip if that side is hanging.

**[Reclined Twist](/poses/reclined-twist)** closes it, undoing the compression a run puts through the spine, and then windshield wipers to let the hips shake loose before you stand up.

Between each one there's a seated pause. That's not a gap in the class — it's where a lot of the relief actually lands, and it's worth sitting through rather than rushing past.

The thing Katie says in the middle of Sleeping Swan is the whole idea: *this is the opposite of running. Right now you are asked to do less.*

If you'd rather practise it without listening to me, the [same three shapes are on the site](/routines/tight-hips-after-running/) with photos and a timer on screen.
