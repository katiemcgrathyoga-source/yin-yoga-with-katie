Three poses. Five minutes in each. That's the class, and the length is the point of it.

Katie says as much before anything starts: holding a shape for five minutes is physically and mentally hard, often frustrating, sometimes just boring — and you finish stronger than you started, because you've spent five minutes not listening to the voice telling you to stop.

But she puts the other half next to it, which is the part most classes leave out. **Value comes from learning when to quit as much as it does from learning not to.** Coming out of a pose that isn't for you today is the practice too.

**[Child's Pose](/poses/childs-pose)** goes first, five minutes, which is longer than it sounds. Build it to last — cushion under the hips, blanket under the knees — or lie on your back and hug the knees in, which is the same shape with the weight taken out.

**[Dragon](/poses/dragon)** is five minutes a side, with a Downward Dog between. The back knee sets the depth. Blocks or books under the hands raise the floor. And close your eyes, because watching your own fingers is what makes you fidget.

**[Happy Baby](/poses/happy-baby)** for five, half or full, with the halfway point called so you can swap. Then **[Corpse](/poses/corpse)**.

Eighty per cent of your capacity throughout. There is no impressive version of any of these, and she says so.
