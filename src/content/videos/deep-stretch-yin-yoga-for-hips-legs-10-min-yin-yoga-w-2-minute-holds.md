Two shapes, done on both sides, and that's the whole ten minutes.

**Half [Shoelace](/poses/shoelace)** first, for two minutes — one foot drawn in beside the opposite hip, knees roughly stacked, the bottom leg long. You'll feel it down the back of the bottom leg and around the outer hip. Sit up on a block or cushion if the hips are tight; it makes a real difference.

**[Deer](/poses/deer)** next, for ninety seconds. Both legs at right angles, one shin in front and one swung behind — Katie mentions its other name, 1990 pose, which is easier to remember. Don't chase the alignment; it looks different on everyone. And it can feel like nothing at first, so let your weight drop into the hips rather than propping yourself up.

Then, without leaving Deer, lean towards the folded-back leg and draw that foot in for the quad. That's the "legs" part of the title.

The same again on the other side, some windshield wipers, done.

Short, seated, no props, and it reaches internal rotation in the hip — which most hip sequences never touch.
