A class for a tired body and a tired mind, and the first twenty minutes are spent face down.

**[Crocodile](/poses/crocodile)** opens it — cheek on your hands, big toes together, heels flopping out. Katie's cue is to make yourself cozy, like sliding into bed after a long day.

**[Sphinx](/poses/sphinx)** for three minutes, or however long you can, with the elbows sliding forward if the lower back objects. Crocodile again, looking the other way this time. **[Seal](/poses/seal)** for a minute if the deeper backbend appeals, and Crocodile once more.

**[Cat Pulling its Tail](/poses/cat-pulling-its-tail)** is the interesting one — a reclined twist with the top foot held behind you, so it reaches the quad and the front of the hip along with the spine. Three minutes each side, with a cushion under the back if the shoulders won't reach the floor.

Then **[knees hugged to the chest](/poses/wind-relieving)**, a one-minute **[reclined twist](/poses/reclined-twist)** each way, and a quiet close.

Somewhere in the middle of it, Katie says the thing the class is really about: it's okay to feel tired, and it's okay not to be productive. Whatever needs doing will get done — and you'll do it better having rested.
