A morning class that never asks you to stand up.

**[Child's Pose](/poses/childs-pose)** for two minutes to start, knees close together so the back rounds, a cushion under the hips if they're complaining. Then the knees go wide, the hands reach forward for the shoulders, and you walk both hands over to one side for a minute and then the other — a side bend through the ribs, which after a night lying still is usually where the stiffness has settled.

**[Sphinx](/poses/sphinx)** for three minutes. This is a strong backbend first thing, so the elbows slide as far forward as you need, and lying flat on your belly is an honest version of it.

Then **[Crocodile](/poses/crocodile)**, and while you're there the legs get some attention — reaching back for one foot at a time for the quad, then holding the ankle and circling the foot both ways, toes spread wide. Ankles wake up stiff and almost nothing addresses them.

**[Seal](/poses/seal)** for a minute if the deeper backbend is welcome, Crocodile again with the other cheek down, and a few quiet breaths sitting up.

The whole thing is deliberately unhurried. It's twenty minutes for a body that isn't ready to be efficient yet.
