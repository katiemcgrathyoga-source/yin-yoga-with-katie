Class 12 of **Creating Peaceful Moments**, and it takes its time. The first ten minutes are a settling meditation and **4:8 breathing** — in for four, out for eight — before a single shape.

**[Sphinx](/poses/sphinx)** for five minutes, elbows under or in front of the shoulders but never behind them, chest shining forward. **[Crocodile](/poses/crocodile)** to rest. **[Seal](/poses/seal)** for two if the deeper backbend appeals, Crocodile again.

Then the long piece: a **[reclined twist](/poses/reclined-twist)** on each side, with **[Cat Pulling its Tail](/poses/cat-pulling-its-tail)** offered as the deeper version — bottom leg bent, that foot caught behind you, top leg out to the side. Three minutes a side.

**[Half Butterfly](/poses/half-butterfly)** for three minutes each way, and afterwards a **rainbow reach** overhead into the side of the ribcage, which a forward fold never touches. A **[seated twist](/poses/seated-twist)** each way.

Then something most Yin classes skip entirely: several minutes of **neck and shoulder work** — fingers interlaced behind the back, one elbow drawn back, ear towards the shoulder, then the throat, then each side.

Five minutes in **[Corpse](/poses/corpse)** to close.

Her line early on is the one that sets the hour up: you have arrived at your mat, the hardest part is over, and all there is to do now is be.
