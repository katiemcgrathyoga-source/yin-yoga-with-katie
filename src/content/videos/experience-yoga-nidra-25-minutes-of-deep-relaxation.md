No poses in this one. You lie down, get warm, and listen.

Katie sets the frame before anything starts, and it's the part worth keeping: **nothing is supposed to happen in Yoga Nidra.** Nothing is good or bad, nothing is right or wrong. Your job is only to welcome whatever turns up, recognise it for what it is — a thought, a feeling, a sound — and witness it.

It opens with **broad awareness**: something outside you, then something inside you, then both at once. Then a **sankalpa**, a short intention in the present tense, which you'll be asked for again near the end.

The long middle is the **rotation of consciousness** — attention moved piece by piece through the body. Mouth, eyes, forehead, face, the back of the head. One arm, then the hand, then each finger, then the other. Chest, belly, back, pelvis. Each leg, each foot, each toe.

Then the **opposites**: feeling very large, then very small, then somehow both. It isn't supposed to add up.

Your sankalpa once more, broad awareness again, and a count back from five.

Get properly comfortable first. Blankets, pillows, an eye pillow if you have one. Twenty-five minutes is a long time to be slightly cold.
