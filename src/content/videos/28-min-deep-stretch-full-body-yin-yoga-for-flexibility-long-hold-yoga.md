The bigger shapes, held two to three minutes, with a gentler version offered every single time. Katie puts it at advanced beginner to intermediate — but if you take the easier option in each pose, it's a beginner class that happens to go somewhere.

**[Frog](/poses/frog)** opens it, three minutes into the inner thighs and groin, built up in stages from **Extended [Child's Pose](/poses/childs-pose)** so you can stop at whatever stage is enough. Pad the knees. Then a minute back in Child's Pose to let it land.

**[Puppy](/poses/puppy)** takes the upper back, shoulders and chest for two minutes, released with a round of Cat.

**[Dragon](/poses/dragon)** is two minutes a side into the front of the hip, with Baby Dragon — front foot between the hands — as the lighter version, and a Downward Dog between sides.

**[Saddle](/poses/saddle)** is the one that asks the most: quads and the front of the hip, upright or leaning back on the hands or elbows or all the way down, whichever you reach honestly. Half Saddle, one leg at a time, is the version most people want, and Katie calls the halfway point so you can swap.

**[Happy Baby](/poses/happy-baby)** for two minutes, then **[Corpse](/poses/corpse)**, which she's firm about — with flexibility work especially, the rest at the end is part of the practice rather than the end of it.
