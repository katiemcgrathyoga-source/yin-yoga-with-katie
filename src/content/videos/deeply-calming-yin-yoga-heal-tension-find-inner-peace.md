An hour built around the kidney meridian line, which Katie explains before anything starts — and which you don't need to believe in for the class to work on you.

The line runs from the little toe, up the inner leg, to the tailbone and up the spine. So the practice is two things: **backbends** and **inner thighs**.

**[Sphinx](/poses/sphinx)** for four minutes with a cue worth keeping — a turtle extending its neck out of its shell, rather than collapsing into the shoulders. Then a minute face down in **[Crocodile](/poses/crocodile)**.

**[Saddle](/poses/saddle)** for three minutes, propped as high as your knees need, or half saddle one leg at a time.

**[Sphinx](/poses/sphinx) or [Seal](/poses/seal)** for five, your choice, and Crocodile again the other way.

Then the inner leg. **[Butterfly](/poses/butterfly)** for four minutes, **[Half Butterfly](/poses/half-butterfly)** for three each side, **[Dragonfly](/poses/dragonfly)** — she calls it Straddle — for three, with windshield wipers between each.

**[Caterpillar](/poses/caterpillar)** for five, countered with Hammock, then **[Corpse](/poses/corpse)**.

The image that makes the backbends make sense: holding one is kinking a garden hose. The point isn't the kink. It's the rush when you let go.
