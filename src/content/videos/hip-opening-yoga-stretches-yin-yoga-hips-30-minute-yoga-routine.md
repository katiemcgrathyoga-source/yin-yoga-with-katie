One round of three hip shapes, done on the right, then done again on the left. That's the whole class, and it's a good way to feel how different your two sides are.

**[Butterfly](/poses/butterfly)** opens it for three minutes — upright or folded forward, feet close in for more or further away for less — then windshield wipers to let the hips move again.

Then the round. **[Dragon](/poses/dragon)** takes the front of the hip for two minutes, the back knee sliding closer or further to set the depth. **[Sleeping Swan](/poses/sleeping-swan)** follows straight from it, walking the same foot across the mat, and reaches the glute and deep outer hip. **[Shoelace](/poses/shoelace)** finishes the set on the outside of both hips, half or full, upright or folded. Wipers again, then the whole thing on the other side.

**[Happy Baby](/poses/happy-baby)** for three minutes, half if you'd rather, and two minutes in **[Corpse](/poses/corpse)**.

The three principles Katie names at the start are worth carrying into it: find your edge, commit to stillness, allow time. About 80% is the edge she asks for — and if you've gone past it, come back.
