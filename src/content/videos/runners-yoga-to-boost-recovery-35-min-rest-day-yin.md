This is the Yin one — and Katie opens it with a piece of guidance worth reading before you press play.

## Not straight after your run

> "Yin yoga is a practice that we do on cold muscles. So if you have just gotten back from a run and your muscles are still warm, then this practice is not the best for you right now… This video is more designed for a rest day, or for a couple of hours after a run."

That's the traditional Yin position: warm, elastic muscle absorbs the stretch, so a long hold on cool tissue is what reaches the deeper connective tissue underneath. If you've just walked in the door, the [10-minute cool-down](/videos/after-running-yoga-10-min-post-run-stretches) is the better fit. Save this one for a rest day, an evening, or a few hours later.

## Three minutes a shape

Yin usually asks for three to five minutes; here it's **around three**, which is long enough to be interesting and short enough to be doable. Have a cushion or a block nearby — Katie offers them under the hips and under the knees throughout, and using one is what lets you stay.

## What you'll do

**Butterfly** to open, soles together, letting gravity take the knees rather than pushing them — with folded Butterfly as the deeper option. Then down one side: **Dragon** for the front of the hip, **Sleeping Swan** for the glute and outer hip, **Half Shoelace**, and **Shoelace** stacking the knees. The whole chain repeats on the second side, then **Dangling** — a standing fold, knees soft — before a seated close.

Five shapes, both sides, thirty-five minutes. Nothing forced, and the further your feet sit from you in Butterfly, the gentler it stays.
