faq:
  - q: "Why three positions instead of one long hold?"
    a: "Because they reach different tissue. Leg up finds the hamstring, leg out to the side finds the inner thigh, and leg across the body finds the outer hip and the band down the side of the leg. It is the same amount of time as one long hold, and it covers three times the ground."
  - q: "Do I have to move straight from one to the next?"
    a: "Yes, and that is the point. The leg is already warm and already at its edge; coming out and setting up again loses everything you just spent two minutes finding. The timer rings a bell at each change so you can keep your eyes closed."
  - q: "What if I do not have a strap?"
    a: "A belt, a dressing-gown tie, a towel or a resistance band all work. If you have nothing at all, hold behind your thigh instead and let the knee bend as much as it likes — the shape still works, you simply have less reach."
  - q: "Is this only for runners?"
    a: "No. Running is what most often leaves the back of the legs short and cross, so runners feel it most, but anyone who sits all day will recognise the same tightness. Nothing here asks for any flexibility you do not already have."
why: "Running and sitting both shorten the back of the leg, and both do it unevenly — the hamstring, the inner thigh and the outer hip all end up involved. Taking one supported shape through three positions reaches all of them in the time a single hold would take, and doing it lying down means the lower back and neck never have to work for it."
