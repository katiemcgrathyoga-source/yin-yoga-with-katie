steps:
  - pose: "dragon"
    seconds: 180
    sides: 2
    note: "Low lunge, back knee down. Let the hips sink. Keep the feeling in the hip, not the knee."
  - pose: "sleeping-swan"
    seconds: 150
    sides: 2
    note: "Front shin across the mat, fold over it and rest. The glutes and the outer hip."
  - pose: "deer"
    seconds: 90
    sides: 2
    note: "One shin in front, one behind. Sit tall, that's the whole pose. Kind to the knees."
  - pose: "reclined-twist"
    seconds: 120
    sides: 2
    note: "Knees drop to one side, shoulders heavy. Undo the run's compression through the spine."
faq:
  - q: "When should I do this hip routine?"
    a: "After a run while you're warm, or on a rest day. Long, deep holds right before a run can leave the hip feeling loose rather than springy, so keep anything pre-run short and moving."
  - q: "How often do runners need to do this?"
    a: "A couple of times a week, kept up for a few weeks, does more than one heroic session. You'll feel the difference after one practice, and it fades over a few days. This tissue responds to time, not effort."
  - q: "What if I only have ten minutes?"
    a: "Do the first two shapes on their own. Dragon and Sleeping Swan reach the front and the back of the hip, and that's most of the work."
  - q: "My front knee hurts in Sleeping Swan. What do I do?"
    a: "Bring the shin closer to your body, or slide a cushion under the hip on that side so it isn't hanging. If the knee still complains, do Deer instead and stay upright."
