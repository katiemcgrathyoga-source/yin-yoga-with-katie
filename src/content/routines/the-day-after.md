faq:
  - q: "Should I stretch when my legs are sore?"
    a: "Gently, yes — but not to fix the soreness. Stretching hasn't been shown to prevent or cure delayed-onset muscle soreness, so anything promising that is overselling. What gentle movement does do is keep you from stiffening up around it, and it feels considerably better than lying still all day."
  - q: "How long does soreness after a run last?"
    a: "It usually turns up 12 to 24 hours after the effort, peaks somewhere around 24 to 48 hours, and eases off after that. If it's still there at day four, or it's sharp rather than achy, that's worth a physio's opinion rather than another stretch."
  - q: "Can I run on sore legs?"
    a: "An easy run often makes ordinary soreness feel better, oddly enough. But sore is different from painful — if something is sharp, one-sided, or changes how you move, take the day."
  - q: "Do I need props for this?"
    a: "No, just a bit of clear wall. A cushion under the head is welcome, and a blanket over you for the last few minutes if you're the sort who gets cold lying still."
  - q: "Is this different from a post-run stretch?"
    a: "Yes — this is for the next morning, not for straight after. Everything is done lying down, because sore legs don't want to kneel, and nothing goes deep, because deep isn't what a recovering muscle is asking for."
