faq:
  - q: "Can you stretch your IT band?"
    a: "Not really, no. The IT band is a thick sheet of fascia running down the outside of the thigh, and it is far too stiff to lengthen in any meaningful way with a stretch. What you can change is the tension in the muscles attaching to it — the tensor fasciae latae at the hip, the glutes behind it and the quads in front. That is what this practice works on."
  - q: "Will this help pain on the outside of my knee?"
    a: "It may ease the everyday tightness that contributes to it. But pain on the outside of the knee while running is worth a physiotherapist's opinion rather than a stretching routine — especially if it is sharp, gets worse as you run, or only happens on one side."
  - q: "When should I do this?"
    a: "After a run while you are still warm, or on a rest day. Not immediately before a run — long, still holds can leave your legs feeling a little loose and less powerful for the first mile."
  - q: "Do I need any props?"
    a: "No. A cushion under the back knee in Dragon makes it a great deal kinder, and something to sit on in Deer helps if your hips are tight, but a folded towel does either job."
  - q: "Is foam rolling the IT band better than this?"
    a: "Rolling the band itself is uncomfortable and does not lengthen it either. If rolling helps you, it is almost certainly changing how the tissue feels rather than its length, which is still worth something. These shapes go after the muscles pulling on it, which is the part you can actually influence."
