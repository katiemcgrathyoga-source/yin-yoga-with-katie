faq:
  - q: "Should runners do yoga on rest days?"
    a: "Yes, if it's this kind. A rest day is for recovery, and slow, supported Yin gives the tissue and the nervous system exactly that, without adding any training load. Save the moving, flowing kind for another day."
  - q: "Will this help with sore legs?"
    a: "It will help the day feel more livable. Legs up the wall gets the blood moving, the twist and the side stretch unclench the bracing that soreness creates, and the long holds settle you. It won't erase the soreness, and nothing does."
  - q: "What do I need for this routine?"
    a: "A bit of wall, and something to lie back over for Supported Fish and Saddle. A bolster is lovely, a rolled blanket or a couple of folded towels does the same job. A cushion under each knee in the first pose is worth finding."
  - q: "Saddle hurts my knees. What do I do?"
    a: "Stay upright, sitting between the heels, or sit up on a block so there's less bend in the knee. If even that is too much, skip it and lie back over the bolster with the legs long instead. Saddle should live in the front of the thighs, never the knee."
  - q: "Can I do this the night before a race?"
    a: "The wall, yes, any time. Keep Saddle, the twist and the side stretch gentle in race week, and stay well away from your end range. Calm is the goal that night, not depth."
