intro: "Recovery is the half of training most runners skip, and it's the half where the running actually lands. Nothing here asks anything of you. Everything is supported, the holds are long, and the whole thing happens on your back or folded over your legs. Open the chest that running closes, let the back of the body lengthen, wring out the spine, then drain the legs and stop. Plan a nap afterward if you can."
steps:
  - pose: "supported-fish"
    seconds: 300
    note: "Bolster or a rolled blanket along the spine, arms wide. Open what a week of running and sitting closed."
  - pose: "caterpillar"
    seconds: 240
    note: "Fold over straight legs and let the head hang. The whole back line, from heels to neck. Round is fine."
  - pose: "reclined-twist"
    seconds: 180
    sides: 2
    note: "Knees to one side, gaze the other way. Shoulders heavy. Slow."
  - pose: "legs-up-the-wall"
    seconds: 480
    note: "Hips close to the wall, legs up, arms soft. Eight minutes. Let the legs empty out."
  - pose: "corpse"
    seconds: 300
    note: "Legs down, nothing to do. Let it all soak in."
faq:
  - q: "Should runners do yoga on rest days?"
    a: "Yes, if it's this kind. A rest day is for recovery, and slow, supported Yin gives the tissue and the nervous system exactly that, without adding any training load. Save the moving, flowing kind for another day."
  - q: "Will this help with sore legs?"
    a: "It will help the day feel more livable. Legs up the wall gets the blood moving, the twist unclenches the bracing that soreness creates, and the long holds settle you. It won't erase the soreness, and nothing does."
  - q: "What do I need for this routine?"
    a: "A bit of wall, and something to lie over for Supported Fish. A bolster is lovely, a rolled blanket or a couple of folded towels does the same job."
  - q: "Can I do this the night before a race?"
    a: "The wall and the corpse pose, yes, any time. Keep the fold and the twist gentle in race week, and stay well away from your end range. Calm is the goal that night, not depth."
