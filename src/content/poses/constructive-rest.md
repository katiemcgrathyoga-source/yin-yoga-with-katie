benefits:
  - "Lets the lower back release without being asked to stretch — the knees bent takes the pull of the legs off the pelvis, so the spine settles by itself."
  - "Gives the hip flexors a chance to let go after hours of sitting or a long run, simply by not holding them anywhere."
  - "Slows the breath and the heart before a practice begins, so what follows is met by a body that has already downshifted."
  - "Asks nothing of you. The most useful shape in a practice is often the one with no shape to get right."
cues:
  - "Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the mat and about as wide as your mat, heels a comfortable distance from your seat."
  - "Let the knees lean in toward one another until they touch, so the legs hold each other up and your muscles do not have to."
  - "Arms wherever they are happy — by your sides, hands on the belly, or wide."
  - "Do nothing else. Let the floor take the whole weight of you and let the breath find its own pace."
transitions_in:
  - "The first shape of a practice, straight from lying down."
  - "From Corpse, by drawing the feet in toward you."
transitions_out:
  - "Extend the legs long into Corpse."
  - "Hug the knees in, then roll to one side to come up."

cautions:
  - "If lying flat is uncomfortable late in pregnancy, come onto your left side instead, or prop the head and chest higher."
  - "A cushion under the head is welcome if the chin lifts toward the ceiling."
faq:
  - q: "Is Constructive Rest a real pose, or just lying down?"
    a: "Both, and that is rather the point. It is a deliberate resting position used in somatic work and physiotherapy as well as yoga — the bent knees let the lower back and hip flexors release in a way that lying flat does not. Doing nothing on purpose is different from doing nothing."
  - q: "How long should I stay?"
    a: "Two minutes is enough to open a practice. If you are using it on its own after a long day or a hard run, ten or fifteen minutes is lovely."
  - q: "My lower back still feels tight. What can I change?"
    a: "Try walking the feet a little closer to your seat, or a little further away — there is usually one distance where the back gives up. A folded blanket under the head can help too."
