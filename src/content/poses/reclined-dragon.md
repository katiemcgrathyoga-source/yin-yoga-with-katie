hold_time: "3 minutes per side"
hold_seconds: 180
props: ["bolster"]
benefits:
  - "Opens the hip flexors from underneath rather than from a lunge, so gravity does the work and the knees carry none of it."
  - "Reaches the front of the hip that running and sitting both shorten — the tissue that tips the pelvis forward and quietly loads the lower back."
  - "Being on your back, there is no balancing and no weight to hold, which is what lets it be held for three unhurried minutes."
  - "A kind alternative to Dragon for anyone whose knees object to kneeling."
cues:
  - "Lie on your back with a bolster under your pelvis, or along the length of your spine — whichever your body prefers."
  - "Draw one knee in toward your chest and hold it there, gently."
  - "Let the other leg lengthen away along the mat, and let its weight drop toward the floor. The front of that hip is the shape."
  - "Shoulders heavy, jaw soft. Stay three minutes, then swap."
transitions_in:
  - "From Constructive Rest, by drawing one knee in and lengthening the other leg."
  - "From lying over a bolster in Supported Bridge."
transitions_out:
  - "Draw both knees in for a moment, then extend into Corpse."
  - "Straight into the second side."
counterposes: ["wind-relieving", "childs-pose"]
cautions:
  - "If the lower back arches uncomfortably, lower the prop or bend the extended leg slightly — this shape should be felt at the front of the hip, never in the back."
  - "Ease off if there is any pinching at the front of the hip socket."
faq:
  - q: "How is this different from Dragon?"
    a: "Same target, opposite relationship with gravity. Dragon is a low lunge where you kneel and sink forward; Reclined Dragon has you on your back with the leg lengthening away, so the floor holds you and the knee carries nothing. It suits sensitive knees, and it suits the end of a practice when you would rather not be upright."
  - q: "Do I need a bolster?"
    a: "No, but it helps. Without one, lie flat, draw one knee in and press the back of the other thigh gently toward the mat — you will feel it, just less. A cushion under the pelvis tilts things so the front of the hip opens further without effort."
  - q: "How long each side?"
    a: "Three minutes is the working length in a restorative practice. Two is plenty if you are new to it."
