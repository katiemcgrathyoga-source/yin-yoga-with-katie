benefits:
  - "A long, supported release for the hamstrings and calves, with the floor taking all the weight so the whole back of the leg can soften."
  - "Because you're lying down, the lower back and neck stay completely at ease — a gentler way to open the hamstrings than a seated forward fold."
  - "A strap does the reaching for you, so there's no rounding or straining to hold on."
  - "Calming and grounding — an easy shape to stay in and breathe."
cues:
  - "Come as you are. Lie on your back with both knees bent, feet on the floor."
  - "Hug one knee in, loop a strap (or a belt or towel) around the ball of that foot, and hold an end in each hand."
  - "Slowly extend the leg up toward the ceiling, only as far as the hamstring happily allows — a soft bend in the knee is completely fine and often kinder."
  - "Let your shoulders, head and hips stay heavy on the mat. Walk your hands down the strap only to the point of a real but comfortable stretch — about 60–80% of your edge."
  - "The lower leg can stay bent with the foot on the floor, or extend along the mat for a little more — whichever keeps your lower back at ease."
  - "Relax your hands, your jaw and your belly. Settle in and breathe."
  - "When the back of the leg has had its time, take both ends of the strap into the hand on that same side and let the leg open out to the side, resting toward the floor. Keep the opposite hip heavy on the mat. This finds the inner thigh."
  - "Then swap the strap into the other hand and let the leg travel across your body instead, knee soft, both shoulders staying down. This finds the outer hip and the side of the leg."
  - "Three parts, one after another with no rest between them. Then take the whole thing on the second side."
transitions_in:

transitions_out:

cautions:
  - "Keep a soft bend in the knee — never lock it straight, especially if you're very flexible or have hypermobile joints."
  - "If your lower back lifts or complains, bend the resting leg and place that foot on the floor to keep the pelvis grounded."
  - "A broad, spreadable stretch down the back of the leg is soft-tissue tension — safe to explore slowly and patiently. A sharp, pulling or electric feeling behind the knee or down the leg is a nerve signal, not a stretch: ease straight off if you feel it."
  - "Naturally tight hamstrings are completely normal — for athletic or very muscular bodies they're meant to be springy and strong. Work gently rather than chasing a deeper shape; length comes slowly, if at all, and that's absolutely fine."
faq:
  - q: "Do I need a strap for the reclined hamstring stretch?"
    a: "A strap makes it much easier and more restful — it does the reaching so you can lie back and relax rather than straining to hold your foot. A belt, dressing-gown tie or towel works just as well."
  - q: "How long should I hold it?"
    a: "About three and a half minutes per side, split across the three parts — roughly a minute and a half with the leg up, then a minute out to the side, then a minute across the body. Move straight from one to the next without resting in between; the leg is already warm, and the pause is what breaks the thread. Then do the whole thing on the second side."
  - q: "Why three parts instead of one long hold?"
    a: "Because the back of the leg is not one tissue. Taking the leg up reaches the hamstring, out to the side reaches the inner thigh, and across the body reaches the outer hip and the IT band. Same strap, same lying-down ease, three different lines — and it costs you no more time than a single long hold would."
  - q: "What if my hamstrings are really tight?"
    a: "Bend the raised knee generously and hold the strap higher up — you'll still get a deep, useful release. Flexibility comes with time and patient, regular practice, never from pushing."
  - q: "Is it okay to keep my knee bent?"
    a: "Absolutely — a soft bend in the knee isn't cheating. The back of the leg still releases, and bending the knee actually lets the hip fold more freely, so the stretch stays comfortable and useful. Straightening the leg is simply an option for a little more, never a requirement. Aim for a steady, spreadable stretch rather than a sharp pull."
